TL;DR — the only interesting question is what works for both humans and ai. Take a holistic view of limitations and opportunities for both. Adapt institutions as well.

A. Only Human-AI-Hybrid Work Matters

Let us just look at the three cases

    1. Human only work.
    1. Human - AI hybrid work
    1. AI only work.

Reflections

    1. it is not subject of this blog. If AI does not affect the work - We don't need to think about it in context of AI.
    1. We need to figure out how to get most of it, and actually make it work - for humans and ai-systems.
    1. if it is truly autonomous ai work, it will either be restricted ie. no access to it, or a commodity everybody gets the same value for same price. Nothing to think about.

Pitfalls

The first: refusing to use AI at all. You write do everything yourself, slowly, at great cost, producing work that — had you used AI well — could have been done faster and at higher quality. The tool exists; not using it is not a virtue.

ie. carrying every brick manually to build your house can be a personal achievement, but as a society we are better off using trucks.

The second: treating AI as a vending machine. You put in a prompt, you take out an output, you ship it. No judgment applied. The human is a middleman, not a contributor. The work is indistinguishable from what anyone else could produce with the same prompt.

ie. students make ai solve homework, completely missing the point and gaining no new knowledge.

Neither is the point. The point is the hybrid. Ai is a tool there are opportunities to use it appropriately, inappropirately - and a lot of questions about how to make it work.

What hybrid actually looks like in practice

The human contribution concentrates in a few high-leverage moments:

  • Defining the problem correctly
  • Setting constraints the model can't infer
  • Reviewing output for contextual correctness, not just technical correctness
  • Making the judgment calls that require knowing things the model doesn't
  • Actual persistent learning and understanding.
  • Expert knowledge and craft.

The AI contribution fills in the space between those moments — speed and efficiency in writing, code generation, research and formatting. Fast, competent, tireless.

The ratio of AI words to human words in the final output tells you almost nothing. What matters is whether human judgment shaped the parts that required judgment, and can keep doing so sustainably.

Counter-arguments

1. "If the output is good, why does it matter who contributed what?" It does not. We will have AI-only work. The point is that it is uninteresting. We don't think about turbines running - we think about who will have to maintain them, the price of electricity etc. We only need to focus on interaction between humans and AI. Both individually and collaborative in corporations and society.

2. "Most work doesn't require that level of judgment." I believe most does. The tasks that seem purely mechanical often have hidden judgment calls: which format to use, which information to include or omit, what world context users or audience require. Fully delegating them to AI doesn't eliminate the judgment — it just means the model made the call, using the model defaults.

3. "This is just a higher bar for using AI. Why frame it as a belief?" Because the default behavior — using AI to produce output without applying judgment — is very easy and very common. The belief is corrective, and forces us to ask different questions. Ie. reviewing 16 running agents might be optimal for agent system, but unsustainable for humans. how do we design our companies and our policies. And how do in practice work with AI as individuals and groups from a holistic human-ai perspective.
