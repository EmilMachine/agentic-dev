TL;DR — The model generates suggestions. You decide what ships. Authorship, copyright, and responsibility all belong to the human. This is a feature, not a limitation.

F. AI Owns Nothing — You Own It All

The model is not an author, a co-author, or a collaborator in any legally meaningful sense. It cannot be held responsible for anything. You, on the other hand, can.

"The computer is incredibly fast, accurate, and stupid. Man is unbelievably slow, inaccurate, and brilliant. Together they are powerful beyond imagination." — Leo Cherne (often misattributed to Einstein, which is itself a good AI parable)

The combination is where the value is. But the brilliant half is still you.

Authorship and responsibility

Copyright: AI-generated content has no copyright holder — courts have been skeptical of granting it to the person who typed the prompt. Your authorship lives in the creative direction, curation, and judgment you applied. The more human decision-making went into the work, the clearer the claim.

Responsibility: If AI-generated code ships a bug that costs a customer money, you are responsible. "The AI wrote it" is not a defense in any court, employment contract, or customer relationship. Your name is on the commit. Your name is on the PR. These aren't symbolic — they reflect the only place where judgment, context, and consequences actually meet.

A small story about credit

A designer used AI to generate ten logo concepts for a client. She selected one, refined it substantially, and the client loved it. When asked if she designed it herself, she said: "I designed the outcome. An AI generated a starting point I chose to develop."

The client was satisfied. More importantly, she was accurate. She owned the creative direction, the selection, the refinement, and the relationship. The model owned none of it, and was perfectly happy about that.

This is a feature

The model did the boring part. You did the interesting part: defined the problem, evaluated the solution, made the call. Craftsmanship used to include the writing; now it includes the judging. A good editor makes an author better without becoming the author. You are the editor now.

Counter-arguments

"The liability exposure is enormous — why use AI at all?" The exposure isn't new. You were always responsible for what you shipped. AI changes the source of the code, not the accountability structure. See Belief C.

"AI-generated code is often better than what I'd write. Shouldn't that reduce my liability?" Quality and liability are separate. A high-quality output you didn't understand is still an unmanaged risk. A lower-quality output you fully understood is a risk you managed. Understanding is what changes the risk profile.

"The 'you own it' framing discourages people from using AI." It clarifies the deal: you get leverage, speed, and a capable assistant; you keep responsibility. Most professionals are already comfortable with that bargain — they're responsible for their work even when tools and colleagues contributed.
